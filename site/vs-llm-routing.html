Routing with an LLM vs a 7M typed router

The common way to turn natural language into a route is prompt a big model and parse JSON or a tool call. It works — and for some teams it's the right answer. This page is the honest trade-off, with our numbers where we have them and gaps marked where we don't.

Pick the router when

  • Volume is high and marginal cost matters — 100k routing calls/day on an LLM bill is real money; on a 7M head it's a CPU that was already on.
  • Requests contain personal data and can't egress (municipal, banking, support tickets).
  • A wrong route is worse than no route — you need measured abstention, not a prompt that says "be careful".
  • The taxonomy is stable enough to invest an afternoon in — then it runs for ~€0 forever.

Stick with the LLM when

  • Routes change weekly — prompt edits beat retraining cadence.
  • You need the model to answer, not just route (summarize, draft, extract) — the router deliberately delegates those.
  • Volume is low and the vendor bill is noise.
  • Nobody owns the taxonomy yet — explore with an LLM first, freeze it into a lane when it stabilizes.

The hybrid we actually recommend

The router isn't competing with your LLM — it sits in front of it. Every route decision can delegate upstream (ANVIL_DELEGATE_URL): confident in-scope → cheap tool call; uncertain/out-of-scope → your LLM, where a big model's knowledge is genuinely needed. You pay LLM prices only for the fraction of traffic that deserves it — typically the clarification tail, not the routed bulk.
